# AT-1 — Security Awareness and Training Policy and Procedures

**Source:** [ITSG-33 Annex 3A, AT-1](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/annex-3a-security-control-catalogue-itsg-33).

## Control definition

Develop, document, and distribute an awareness and training policy and supporting procedures to designated people or roles. The policy covers purpose, scope, responsibilities, management commitment, coordination, and compliance. Review and update both policy and procedures on organisation-defined schedules.

**Organisation-defined parameters to review:** recipients/roles and review frequency for the policy and procedures. **Suggested values for review:** make the policy available to all workforce members and relevant contractors; review annually and after material changes to systems, threats, or requirements.

## Supplemental guidance

The policy and procedures establish how the organisation implements the Awareness and Training family. They should reflect applicable legislation and government policy, and may be part of the organisation-wide security program rather than duplicated for each system. The organisation’s risk strategy determines the level of system-specific detail.

## In plain language

This is the staff handbook for security learning: it says who needs to learn what, who is responsible, and when the handbook should be refreshed.

## Stagehand initial assessment

Stagehand documentation can explain product-specific safe operation, but cannot establish or administer an adopting organisation’s security training program. Operators should incorporate Stagehand into existing security awareness policies, covering approved use, data handling, browser permissions, credential hygiene, automation risks, reporting errors or incidents, and prohibited workflows. Assign an accountable owner and keep the instructions current as deployment modes change.
