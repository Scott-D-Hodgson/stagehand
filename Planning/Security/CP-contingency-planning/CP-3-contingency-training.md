# CP-3 — Contingency Training

**Source:** [ITSG-33 Annex 3A, CP-3](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/annex-3a-security-control-catalogue-itsg-33).

## Control definition

Train users for their contingency roles within an organisation-defined period after assuming them, when system changes require it, and at an organisation-defined recurring frequency.

**Organisation-defined parameters to review:** time to initial role training and recurring frequency. **Suggested values for review:** train before an individual performs a contingency duty; refresh annually and after material plan changes.

## Supplemental guidance

Training detail should match assigned responsibilities. General users may need reporting and fallback instructions; administrators may need alternate-site recovery steps; managers may need communications and mission-continuity procedures.

## In plain language

A fire plan only works if everyone knows their role: occupants, responders, and managers each practice different tasks.

## Stagehand initial assessment

Train users and operators how to stop running automation, preserve required evidence, switch to approved manual workflows, restore plans from trusted copies, and report suspected compromise. Train administrators on recovery of runner/browser environments and safe credential rotation. Tie content to the actual contingency plan.

## Control enhancements

1. **CP-3(1), simulated events:** incorporate simulated incidents into contingency training.
2. **CP-3(2), automated training environments:** use automated mechanisms to provide realistic contingency training environments.
